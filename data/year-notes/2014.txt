
 1. Political Landmark: The 16th Lok Sabha Elections

The event that dominated 2014 was the massive shift in India's central governance during the general elections held in nine phases across April and May.
• A Historic Mandate: The Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP) won a landslide victory, securing 282 seats independently. This marked the first time in 30 years that a single political party achieved a full majority without relying on a coalition.
• New Leadership: On May 26, 2014, Narendra Modi was officially sworn in as the 15th Prime Minister of India, succeeding Dr. Manmohan Singh.
• The Delhi Shuffle: Earlier in February, Arvind Kejriwal resigned as the Chief Minister of Delhi after a brief 49-day tenure following the deadlock over the Jan Lokpal Bill, plunging the capital into President's Rule before subsequent state elections.

🗺️ 2. Geopolitical Shifts: Formation of Telangana

• India's 29th State: Following decades of intense localized struggle and the passing of the Andhra Pradesh Reorganisation Act, Telangana officially became India's 29th state on June 2, 2014.
• K. Chandrashekar Rao took charge as its very first Chief Minister, while Hyderabad was designated as the shared capital for both states for a transition period of up to ten years.

🚀 3. Science & Health Milestones

• Mars Orbiter Mission (Mangalyaan): On September 24, 2014, India created global space history. The Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO) successfully inserted the Mangalyaan spacecraft into Martian orbit. India became the first nation in the world to successfully reach Mars on its very first attempt, and the first Asian country to do so.
• Polio-Free India: In March 2014, the World Health Organization (WHO) officially certified India—and the entire South-East Asian region—as completely Polio-Free, celebrating a monumental victory for public health campaigns.

📢 4. Launch of Landmark National Campaigns

Following the change in governance, the central government rapidly rolled out public programs that changed the economic and social landscape:
• Pradhan Mantri Jan Dhan Yojana (August 28): A massive financial inclusion drive aimed at ensuring every single household in India has access to a bank account. Millions of zero-balance accounts were opened in record time.
• Make in India (September 25): A global initiative launched to transform India into an international manufacturing hub, encouraging domestic production and inviting foreign investments.
• Swachh Bharat Abhiyan (October 2): Launched on Mahatma Gandhi’s birth anniversary, this nationwide cleanliness campaign focused on eliminating open defecation and modernizing solid waste management.
• NITI Aayog Announcement: On December 31, 2014, Prime Minister Modi announced the complete scrapping of the historic 65-year-old Planning Commission, replacing it with the NITI Aayog (National Institution for Transforming India).

⚖️ 5. Judiciary & Legal Reforms

• Recognition of the Third Gender: In a historic human rights ruling (National Legal Services Authority v. Union of India), the Supreme Court formally recognized transgender individuals as a distinct "third gender," safeguarding their constitutional rights.
• Coal Allocation Scrapped: The Supreme Court sent shockwaves through the corporate sector by declaring the allocation of 214 coal blocks distributed between 1993 and 2010 completely illegal, forcing structural reforms in the energy sector.

🌊 6. Major Natural Disasters

The year saw severe natural anomalies causing widespread humanitarian challenges:
• Kashmir Floods (September): Catastrophic rains caused the Jhelum River to overflow, triggering the worst floods the region had seen in half a century. Hundreds lost their lives, and thousands were stranded before the Indian Armed Forces launched Operation Megh Rahat.
• Cyclone Hudhud (October): A category-4 equivalent severe cyclonic storm battered the eastern coast, causing massive structural and agricultural damage in Visakhapatnam, Andhra Pradesh, and parts of Odisha.

🏆 7. International Laurels

• Nobel Peace Prize: Indian child rights activist Kailash Satyarthi was awarded the Nobel Peace Prize alongside Pakistan's Malala Yousafzai. Satyarthi was recognized for his decades of relentless work through the Bachpan Bachao Andolan, rescuing over 80,000 children from forced labor and trafficking.
